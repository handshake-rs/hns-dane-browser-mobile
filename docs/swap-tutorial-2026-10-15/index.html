What slowed this test down

These captures show where the first attempt and later chain waits needed extra explanation. The 12 steps above follow the completed swap.

  1. A

    The first Bitcoin fee cap was too small

    The separate 1,330-sat attempt accepted terms but could not prepare the complete Bitcoin funding package under its 1,000-sat signed cap. No coins were locked in that attempt. The later 4,330-sat offer used a 4,000-sat cap and funded successfully. In the updated app, this check runs before offer review and shows the estimated minimum cap while there is still time to edit it.

  2. B

    HNS change and reservations temporarily change the balance

    After Moto broadcast its HNS lock, its confirmed spendable balance looked empty while change waited for confirmation. The old unfunded session also held a separate 0.500546-HNS reservation. Check the transaction and the session ID before assuming funds left the wallet.

  3. C

    Follow the settlement transaction through the scan

    Moto briefly showed a generic action error after the secret was verified, even though its Bitcoin claim had been submitted. The claim transaction ID and Bitcoin activity are the reliable way to follow the saved broadcast; the final 5-of-5 state appears after the verified scan.

The amounts, fees, block heights, and session IDs shown here are observations from this two-phone test. Check the live approval on your own wallet before broadcasting a transaction.
